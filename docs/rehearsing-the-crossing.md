# Rehearsing the Crossing

*The sound philosophy of the kirtan machine — and what to build.*

**Draft v1 · 2026-07-07.** Written out of a month with Brian Eno (School of Song, January 2025), read against the lineage this rig sits in (Neem Karoli / Ram Dass). Full workshop catalogue and sources: `~/Desktop/maya/eno-workshop/catalogue/`.

---

## 1. The room

Strip the cosmology out of a contemplative sound practice and see what survives. That experiment has been run, for fifty years, by Brian Eno.

He begins his day by sitting with no input — no phone, no food, no news — and waiting. His words: *"instead of things coming into you, things start coming out of you."* That is pratyahara, described by a man who would never use the word. He treats attention as the contested ground of a life — *shepherd your attention* — which is the whole contemplative project stated as studio advice. He builds music *for* something: music to help a frightened passenger *"surrender a little bit,"* to feel *"less important, less in yourself."* He spent a career taking himself out of the picture — *"I wanted less and less of me in the songs"* — because the voice hijacks attention the way the ego hijacks perception. And he keeps an archive he calls his memory, in which nothing is discarded and everything can return.

A morning sit. Attention as practice. Functional sound for surrender. The dissolution of the self in the work. This is a sadhana. It is rigorous, tested, and complete on its own terms — a map of the entire room that a sound practice can occupy without ever saying a devotional word.

The kirtan machine takes that map seriously. Then it walks to the walls.

## 2. The walls

Wherever Eno goes silent, a devotional technology is standing on the other side of the wall. Four silences matter here.

**Surrender without a beloved.** Eno's surrender aims at equanimity — release of the self toward acceptance. There is no object of love in it. The kirtan tradition reaches the same no-self by the opposite road: not subtracting the self until it thins out, but saturating it with the divine name until there is no room left. Subtraction and saturation — two gestures, one destination. The technology Eno does not have is *nama*: the name, repeated. It is the oldest attention-anchor there is, and it is the one thing his system cannot generate, because a name must be given, not designed.

**Aliveness without ground.** Eno heard the tambura and drew the right conclusion at the level of sound: a drone must have life, its strings phasing, never static — *"these drones had life to them."* The tradition hears one thing more. The drone is tuned to Sa, the tonic that never resolves because it never leaves. Every melody arises out of it and falls back into it. For Eno the drone is a living texture. For nada yoga it is the deathless ground against which all arising and passing becomes audible. Same instrument, one more octave of meaning.

**Time without a wheel.** Eno divides all music into two categories, synchronized and non-synchronized — and it is a genuinely new division. But both of his categories are *lines*. He has no cycle: no avartana turning, no sam — the first beat, the homecoming, where the whole ensemble lands together — and no khali, the marked empty beat where the bass hand lifts and the cycle exhales. The tala is a wheel of departure and return, with a small emptiness built into every revolution. Eno can dissolve you; he cannot bring you home, because his time never comes back around.

**The record, not the room.** Eno's practice ends in an artifact for a solitary listener. He barely performs. Kirtan cannot exist that way — it is sung, clapped, breathed, answered. The wall of voices is made of bodies. Where his practice ends, this one begins.

None of this is a criticism of Eno. It is a use of him. He is the control condition: the most complete secular sound-sadhana ever built. What it lacks, by construction, is a precise inventory of what the devotional traditions are *for*.

## 3. The door

Eno designed *Music for Airports* for a person facing the possibility of dying — his words, not an interpretation: music to make you *"not mind so much the prospect of dying."* Hospitals adopted it, unprompted, for the labor ward. The same object serving both thresholds is not a coincidence. It is structure showing through.

Birth and death are one event seen from opposite sides of the veil. In the language this studio already uses: birth is the soul taking on Maya; death is Maya falling away. Music that helps at both is threshold music — sound for holding the veil lightly when you are not in control. Eno built bardo music without the concept. The Tibetans, who have the concept, read scripture aloud to the dying for exactly this purpose, and in their cosmology death, bardo, and birth are one continuous loop — which is why one piece of music can serve the whole passage.

The tradition holds this as an image: Nataraja, Shiva dancing creation and destruction as a single gesture, on the cremation ground that is also the ground of creation. And it holds it as a sound: the tanpura and the raga. The drone is the ground that does not die. The melody and the tala turning above it are the mortal dance — arising, passing, arising. A raga over a drone is the entire cosmology stated acoustically.

So the door Eno's map stops at, named plainly: **a kirtan is a rehearsal of the crossing.** The voice emerges from the drone (birth), rides the wheel through hundreds of small departures and returns (a life), saturates, dissolves back into the drone (death), and the drone into silence (the ground). Every cycle of the tala is the loop in miniature — a small death at khali, a homecoming at sam. Every repetition of the name is a self let go and taken up again. Practiced weekly, it does what all rehearsal does: makes the real performance less frightening.

This is what the machine is for.

## 4. The instrument

The rig already reaches for this — the scene arc (opening, call/response, building, climax, wind-down, closing into drone and silence) *is* the crossing arc. What follows names the design so the builds can deepen it rather than decorate it.

**Three strata of time, stacked vertically:**

| Stratum | Track | Time | Role |
|---------|-------|------|------|
| **Ground** | Tanpura | None — unclocked, never repeats | The deathless drone. Eno's non-synchronized layer, tuned to Sa. |
| **Wheel** | Tabla / kartals | Cyclic — the avartana, sam and khali audible | Departure and return. The stratum Eno doesn't have. |
| **Voice** | Mic / harmonium | Free — unquantized, stretchable | The one who crosses. Rides above the grid, like breath. |

The Ground runs on Eno's physics (incommensurate cycles, phasing, no downbeat). The Wheel runs on the tradition's physics (the cycle that breathes and returns). The Voice runs on neither — it is live, mortal, unrepeatable. The instrument is the meeting of the two time-systems with a life moving between them.

**Two gestures, one rig.** The Eno gesture: subtract, until only ground remains. The kirtan gesture: saturate — let the voice accumulate, repetition on repetition, until only the name remains. Both empty the center. The rig should make each gesture one motion, and the whole set is one gesture performed with the other's material.

**The voice must accumulate and decay.** A looper on the mic, synced to the avartana, with feedback deliberately below unity: each cycle you sing adds a voice; each cycle you don't, the older voices fade a generation. Sing, and the wall of voices builds — the congregation, conjured by repetition. Stop, and impermanence is in the signal path itself. Nothing sustains that is not renewed. This is the death/birth loop as audio engineering, and it needs no AI to exist. (Later, in the sonic system, each rebirth can change body — a phrase re-voiced through a RAVE timbre model is the same melody in new flesh. Reincarnation is what timbre transfer *is*.)

**The stop must be sudden.** The tradition ends a kirtan the way Fela ends a groove: everything at once, and then the ringing room. One pad kills Wheel and Voice together and leaves the Ground with the reverb tail. Then the Ground goes, and what remains is the thing the whole rig exists to produce: the silence afterward, in a room that has been changed. The silence is the deliverable.

**No headphones.** Sing in front of the speakers. Let the mic hear the room, the tanpura, the bleed. Eno banned headphones for the singer's sake; *Discreet Music* taught him music without edges — sound that admits the world instead of sealing against it. A kirtan is not sealed. The room sings too.

## 5. The build

Sequenced for the instrument track — no vinyl, no AI required until step 5. Each step is playable the day it's done.

1. **Living tanpura** *(~20 minutes, transforms the rig)* — Duplicate the tanpura sample into three clips trimmed to incommensurate lengths (e.g. 61s / 73s / 89s), small pan and EQ offsets, all launched at set start. They never align the same way twice: the drone becomes unrepeating, alive, endless. This is the literal *Music for Airports* mechanism applied to the traditional drone. (Companion spec: sonic #23, phasing-harmonics synth partials, for the MIDI-synth version.)
2. **The breathing cycle** — Program sam and khali into the keherwa clips: fullest stroke on 1, bass hand lifted on 5, a slight reverb-send swell into sam. All three intensity levels keep the shape. The wheel exhales at khali and comes home at sam, every bar.
3. **The voice track** — Shure mic in (the rig currently has no voice channel — the biggest gap). Looper synced to one or two avartanas, feedback ~85–90%, overdub always on. Monitor on speakers. This is the accumulate/decay engine — the core of the instrument.
4. **The sudden stop** — One pad: Wheel and Voice dead, Ground and tail ringing. Rehearse the ending as carefully as the peak.
5. **Reincarnation (sonic track, later)** — RAVE timbre transfer on the looper return (sonic #16): each rebirth of the phrase in a new body, trained on the vinyl corpus — voices of records that are themselves the preserved dead. The ancestors sing the response.

## 6. Coda — where it weaves back

Every session ends in a capture (sonic #18). The captures go to the archive. The archive is Eno's one devotional instinct — *"what's maturing is you"* — and it is the same loop at the scale of a working life: phrases die into it, and a later self, or a machine that has listened longer than any self, brings them back in new bodies. That is the thread that leads from this rig back to the Heart Machine. It gets pulled later. First, the instrument.

*The machine is a rehearsal space for the one crossing everyone makes. Build it so the rehearsal is worth attending.*
